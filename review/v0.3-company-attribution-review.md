# Company attribution review for v0.3

The revised book clearly identifies Assaf Barnir as the person making the judgments and recommendations. Sentry's portrayal is constructive: a developer-product company providing the setting for a personal account of improving support. The book still describes real operational friction and lessons from a migration. It is not an anonymous story, and readers can connect those experiences to the employer.

The review covers the entire current manuscript, including claims that imply company policy or commitments without naming Sentry. It does not change the archived v0.2 edition or prior commits.

## The distinction readers now see

The Reader's Note says:

> This is my personal field guide, shaped by work at Sentry and elsewhere. The judgments and recommendations are mine; I am not speaking for Sentry or describing its official policies. The migration scenes reflect my experience at the time. The playbooks describe how I recommend running the work.

The narrative follows that distinction. Historical observations are framed as the author's experience, while recommendations and ambitions are identified as such. The README carries the same personal-authorship distinction.

## Podcast style

The thank-you to the Thunai team is removed. The feature still names the show and producer, links to YouTube and Spotify, and expresses the author's pleasure in sharing the conversation. That gives the episode a visible place without making the passage read like promotional acknowledgments.

The five chapter contributions now begin with their ideas or stories. They no longer repeat “On the podcast” or “On The Prompt Room.” The version notes, front-matter feature, dated field note, and sources appendix retain their respective navigation and attribution roles.

## Association that remains

- Chapters 1, 11, and 25 still describe historical workflow friction and the effort to improve it. Removing the company name everywhere would not make those experiences unrecognizable.
- Chapter 21 remains a critical account of an unnamed implementation arrangement. Readers familiar with the project may connect it to the migration. The account now describes what the author observed and needed, rather than claiming to know the partner's motives.
- The twelvefold increase in collected customer signals remains an explicitly author-reported observation, not an independently established result or a count of distinct customer problems. The 16% calculation remains illustrative.
- Public podcast and event pages retain their own wording. The archived edition and older commits retain their historical text.

The editorial result separates the author's voice from company representation while preserving the experience that makes the book useful. It does not imply company endorsement or independent verification of every firsthand account.
