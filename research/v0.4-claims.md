## Source checks for the revision

- Rechecked November 2, 2026: [Salesforce's completion announcement](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/09/10/salesforce-completes-acquisition-of-fin/) supports the September 10 acquisition date and the company name Fin, formerly Intercom. It supplies no evidence of the author's customer outcomes.
- Rechecked November 2, 2026: [Fin's announcement](https://fin.ai/blog/introducing-fin-4) supports the October 7 launch and distinguishes Fin's agent from the Intercom Helpdesk. The delayed-outcome reference retains Intercom when naming the helpdesk; renaming the company does not rename every product.
- The preserved First Edition contains the historical CSAT/SLA numbers, manual-tagging account, gala anecdote, build/buy statements, wizard accusation, and speed multiplier quoted by the proposed revision. Their presence in an earlier book is provenance, not independent verification of the underlying results.
- On November 2, 2026, the author confirmed the historical CSAT over 95% and SLA around 98%, thousands of manually tagged conversations, and the gala anecdote. He identified his own deep analysis of historical and current customer experience as the basis of the 5x–12x comparison. The revision attributes that result to his operation; it does not describe it as an unsupported number or a general forecast for other teams.
- Corrections to the first edition describe what its text did or did not explain. They do not invent the author's motives, assert that no supporting data ever existed, or retroactively claim a different release practice.
- For the ships metaphor, [UNAM's history project](https://www.noticonquista.unam.mx/index.php/amoxtli/1410/1410) distinguishes the familiar burning story from the destruction of the ships. The metaphor is not evidence that an operational migration should eliminate recovery.
